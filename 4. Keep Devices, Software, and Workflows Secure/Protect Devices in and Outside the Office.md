### Learning Objective

By the end of this section, we will understand how to protect work devices in the office, at home, while traveling, and in public places.

Employees should learn this core behavior:

**Protect your device like it contains company data, because it probably does.**

Managers and executives should learn this assurance principle:

**The company must be able to secure, locate, lock, wipe, and remove access from work devices when needed.**

### Why This Matters

Laptops, phones, and tablets often contain or can access email, files, customer records, business applications, chats, calendars, passwords, MFA prompts, and confidential documents.

A lost, stolen, unlocked, or unmanaged device can expose company data even if the company’s systems are otherwise secure.

**Device risk is not limited to the office. It follows employees into:**

- Homes
    
- Airports
    
- Hotels
    
- Cars
    
- Cafés
    
- Client sites
    
- Shared workspaces
    
- Conferences
    
- Public transport
    
- Remote work locations
    

Immediately report lost or stolen portable devices to the appropriate authorities, and use password protection, tracking, locking, wiping, and updating mobile devices used for business.

### Core Rule

Keep work devices:

**Locked, updated, physically protected, and reported immediately if lost, stolen, or suspicious.**

The device does not need to be physically stolen to create risk. It may be exposed if someone sees the screen, uses an unlocked session, copies files, photographs information, connects unknown accessories, or accesses it after it is left unattended.

### In the Office

**Employees should:**

1. Lock the screen when stepping away.
    
2. Keep laptops, phones, and tablets under control.
    
3. Avoid leaving sensitive documents on desks.
    
4. Do not let visitors use work devices.
    
5. Do not plug in unknown USB devices.
    
6. Avoid leaving meeting-room screens or shared displays showing confidential information.
    
7. Collect printed documents quickly.
    
8. Report missing devices, badges, or access cards immediately.
    

A device left unlocked for two minutes can be enough for someone to send emails, access files, or copy information.

### At Home

Remote work does not remove company security expectations.

**Employees should:**

1. Use company-approved devices for company work.
    
2. Keep work devices separate from family or shared household use.
    
3. Lock the device when away from it.
    
4. Use a private workspace where possible.
    
5. Avoid leaving confidential documents visible.
    
6. Use approved remote access or VPN if required.
    
7. Keep home routers and Wi-Fi protected with strong passwords.
    
8. Report lost, stolen, damaged, or suspicious devices immediately.
    

Family members, roommates, guests, and children should not use company devices.

### In Public Places

Public spaces increase the risk of observation, theft, and accidental disclosure.

**Employees should:**

1. Avoid working on highly sensitive information in public places.
    
2. Watch for shoulder surfing.
    
3. Use a privacy screen where appropriate.
    
4. Keep devices physically close.
    
5. Never leave devices unattended.
    
6. Avoid discussing confidential information where others can hear.
    
7. Be cautious with public Wi-Fi.
    
8. Use approved secure connections when required.
    
9. Do not plug into unknown charging stations or unknown USB ports.
    

A café or airport is not a private office.

### While Traveling

Travel increases device risk because employees are distracted, mobile, and often using unfamiliar networks.

**Employees should:**

1. Carry devices as hand luggage where possible.
    
2. Avoid leaving devices in cars, hotel rooms, or conference spaces.
    
3. Keep devices locked when not in use.
    
4. Use company-approved chargers and accessories.
    
5. Avoid unknown USB drives, cables, and charging ports.
    
6. Report border, airport, hotel, or theft incidents if company data may be involved.
    
7. Use approved travel procedures for high-risk trips.
    
8. Report lost or stolen devices immediately.
    

For international or high-risk travel, employees should follow the company’s travel-security guidance.

### Lost or Stolen Devices

If a device is lost or stolen, speed matters.

**Employees should:**

1. Report it immediately through the company’s approved process.
    
2. Provide the device type, location, time, and circumstances.
    
3. Say what company systems or data may have been accessible.
    
4. Follow instructions from IT, security, management, or legal.
    
5. Do not wait to see if the device “turns up later.”
    
6. Report theft to local authorities where required.
    

Fast reporting may allow the company to lock the device, wipe it, revoke access, reset sessions, or monitor for misuse.

### What Employees Should Do

**Employees should:**

- Lock devices when unattended.
    
- Use strong device PINs, passwords, or biometrics.
    
- Keep devices physically secure.
    
- Use approved devices for company work.
    
- Keep work devices away from unauthorized users.
    
- Be careful in public places.
    
- Use secure remote access when required.
    
- Report lost, stolen, damaged, or suspicious devices immediately.
    
- Follow company travel procedures.
    
- Keep devices updated and restarted when required.
    

### What Employees Should Not Do

**Employees should not:**

- Leave work devices unattended in public
    
- Leave laptops visible in cars
    
- Let family or friends use work devices
    
- Ignore lost or stolen devices
    
- Use unknown USB drives
    
- Connect to suspicious public Wi-Fi without approved protection
    
- Work on sensitive files where others can see
    
- Store work files on personal devices without approval
    
- Disable security tools
    
- Share device PINs or unlock methods
    
- Treat mobile phones as low-risk devices
    

Phones are work devices too if they access company email, files, chats, or MFA.

### Manager Responsibilities

**Managers should:**

- Reinforce device-locking and clean-desk habits.
    
- Support prompt reporting of lost or stolen devices.
    
- Ensure remote staff have approved equipment.
    
- Avoid pressuring staff to use personal devices without approval.
    
- Make sure traveling staff understand device-security expectations.
    
- Escalate repeated device issues.
    
- Ensure departing employees return company devices and access tools.
    

Managers should not treat lost-device reporting as an embarrassment. It is a time-sensitive security action.

### Executive and Key Staff Assurance Points

Executives and key staff should verify that device security is managed, not assumed.

**Leadership should ensure the company has:**

- Device inventory
    
- Mobile device management where appropriate
    
- Disk encryption on laptops
    
- Screen-lock requirements
    
- Remote lock or wipe capability
    
- Lost/stolen device reporting procedure
    
- Endpoint protection coverage
    
- Remote-work device policy
    
- BYOD policy, if personal devices are allowed
    
- Travel-security guidance
    
- Offboarding process for device return and access removal
    

It is highly recommend to encrypt device data as an added protection layer, and enable the ability to track, lock, or wipe lost or stolen smartphones and tablets.

### Questions Leaders Should Ask

Executives and key staff should ask:

1. Do we know which devices access company data?
    
2. Are laptops encrypted?
    
3. Can lost devices be locked or wiped?
    
4. Are mobile devices managed?
    
5. Are screen-lock settings enforced?
    
6. Are personal devices allowed, and under what rules?
    
7. Are remote workers using approved devices?
    
8. Are endpoint protection tools installed and working?
    
9. Is there a clear lost/stolen device reporting process?
    
10. Are devices returned and access removed during offboarding?
    

### Key Takeaway

Work devices carry business risk wherever they go.

Employees should lock devices, protect them physically, avoid public exposure, use approved equipment, and report loss or theft immediately. Managers should support secure remote and travel habits. Executives should verify device inventory, encryption, endpoint protection, remote lock/wipe, and offboarding controls.

**A device outside the office is still inside the company’s risk perimeter.**
