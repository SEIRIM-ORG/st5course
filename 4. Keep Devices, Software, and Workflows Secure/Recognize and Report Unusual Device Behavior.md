### Goal

We learn to report pop-ups, redirects, security warnings, unexpected slowness, missing files, disabled security tools, unusual applications, and suspected malware. Employees should recognize and report unusual behavior, not diagnose malware or attempt their own cleanup.

### Learning Objective

By the end of this section, we will be able to recognize warning signs that a device, application, browser, or account may be compromised and know how to report the issue quickly.

Employees should learn this core behavior:

**If your device behaves unusually, stop risky activity and report it. Do not try to investigate or fix it yourself.**

Managers and executives should learn this assurance principle:

**The company must have a clear way for staff to report suspicious device behavior and a defined process for investigating it.**

### Why This Matters

A device may show warning signs before a serious incident becomes obvious. Malware, unauthorized access, ransomware, unsafe software, browser hijacking, or failed security controls may first appear as small changes in device behavior.

Employees are often the first people to notice that something is wrong.

**Early reporting can help the company:**

- Contain malware
    
- Protect accounts
    
- Stop data loss
    
- Investigate suspicious activity
    
- Prevent spread to other devices
    
- Preserve evidence
    
- Reduce recovery time
    
- Warn other employees
    

The mistake is waiting too long or trying to fix the problem quietly.

### Core Rule

If your work device, browser, application, or security tool behaves unusually:

**Stop. Do not continue sensitive work. Report it through the approved company process.**

Do not assume the problem is harmless just because the device still works.

### Warning Signs Employees Should Recognize

**Employees should report unusual behavior, which security staff might later refer to as 'Indicators of Compromise' such as:**

- Security or antivirus warnings
    
- Unexpected pop-ups
    
- Browser redirects
    
- A homepage or search engine changing by itself
    
- New programs or icons appearing unexpectedly
    
- Applications opening or closing on their own
    
- Files missing, renamed, changed, or encrypted
    
- Unusual slowness or crashes
    
- The device overheating or battery draining unusually fast
    
- Password prompts appearing unexpectedly
    
- MFA prompts the employee did not initiate
    
- Email messages sent from the account without the employee’s knowledge
    
- Contacts receiving strange messages from the employee
    
- Unknown browser extensions
    
- Security tools disabled or showing errors
    
- Strange remote-access prompts
    
- Unexpected requests to install software
    
- Unusual account login alerts
    
- Locked files or ransom messages
    

One warning sign may not prove compromise. But it is enough to report.

### What Employees Should Do

**Employees should:**

1. Stop using the device for sensitive work.
    
2. Do not enter passwords or MFA codes into suspicious prompts.
    
3. Do not approve unexpected MFA prompts.
    
4. Disconnect from external devices if instructed by company policy.
    
5. Keep the device available for IT or security review.
    
6. Take note of what happened, when it started, and what was clicked or opened.
    
7. Report immediately using the approved channel.
    
8. Follow instructions from IT, security, or management.
    

Employees should give clear facts, not guesses.

**Useful details include:**

- What device was affected
    
- What warning appeared
    
- What application or website was open
    
- What link, file, or email was involved
    
- Whether credentials were entered
    
- Whether MFA was approved
    
- Whether files appear missing or changed
    
- When the issue started
    

### What Employees Should Not Do

**Employees should not:**

- Ignore repeated warnings
    
- Keep working on sensitive files
    
- Enter passwords into suspicious prompts
    
- Approve unexpected MFA requests
    
- Try random online fixes
    
- Install cleanup tools from the internet
    
- Delete suspicious emails, files, or logs before reporting
    
- Wipe or reset the device without instruction
    
- Forward suspicious files to coworkers
    
- Move suspected infected files to shared drives
    
- Hide the issue because they clicked something
    
- Assume IT already knows
    

The employee’s job is not to diagnose the problem. The employee’s job is to report quickly.

### Common Sample Scenarios


#### Common Scenario: Security Warning

An employee sees a message from the company’s security software saying a threat was blocked.

**Unsafe response:**

Close the alert and continue working without telling anyone.

**Safe response:**

Report the warning, especially if it followed a clicked link, opened attachment, download, or unusual website.

#### Common Scenario: Strange Browser Behavior

A browser starts redirecting searches to unfamiliar websites and showing pop-ups.

**Unsafe response:**

Download a free browser cleanup tool from the internet.

**Safe response:**

Stop using the browser for work and report the issue.

#### Common Scenario: Files Look Changed

A user notices files have strange names, cannot be opened, or appear encrypted.

**Unsafe response:**

Keep clicking files to see what works.

**Safe response:**

Stop interacting with the files and report immediately. This may be ransomware or file corruption.

#### Common Scenario: Unexpected MFA Prompt

An employee receives MFA prompts even though they are not logging in.

**Unsafe response:**

Approve the prompt to make it stop.

**Safe response:**

Deny the prompt and report it. This may mean someone has the employee’s password.

### Manager Responsibilities

**Managers should:**

- Encourage fast reporting of suspicious device behavior.
    
- Avoid blaming employees for reporting mistakes.
    
- Make sure staff know the reporting channel.
    
- Escalate reports quickly.
    
- Support employees who need to stop work while a device is checked.
    
- Avoid telling staff to “just restart and keep working” when warning signs are serious.
    
- Ensure contractors and remote staff know how to report device issues.
    

Managers should treat unusual device behavior as a security signal, not just an inconvenience.

### Executive and Key Staff Assurance Points

Executives and key staff should verify that suspicious device behavior can be detected, reported, and investigated.

**Leadership should ensure the company has:**

- Endpoint protection on company devices
    
- Security alerts routed to IT or a managed provider
    
- A clear reporting path for employees
    
- A process for handling suspected malware or compromise
    
- Remote support procedures
    
- Logging where appropriate
    
- A way to isolate or disable affected devices
    
- Training that tells employees what to report
    
- A culture where early reporting is rewarded
    
- Post-incident review for recurring issues
    

The leadership question is:

**Can employees report suspicious device behavior quickly, and does someone qualified respond?**

### Questions Leaders Should Ask

**Executives and key staff should ask:**

1. Are all company devices protected by endpoint security?
    
2. Who receives security alerts?
    
3. Are alerts reviewed promptly?
    
4. Do employees know how to report unusual device behavior?
    
5. Is there an emergency reporting channel?
    
6. Can IT or the managed provider isolate affected devices?
    
7. Are remote workers covered?
    
8. Are suspicious device reports tracked?
    
9. Are repeat issues reviewed for root cause?
    
10. Are employees trained not to self-remediate?
    

### Evidence Leaders Should Request

**Useful evidence includes:**

- Endpoint protection coverage report
    
- Security alert routing process
    
- Incident reporting procedure
    
- Help desk escalation workflow
    
- Malware response procedure
    
- Device isolation capability
    
- Remote support process
    
- Security training records
    
- Logs of reported device issues
    
- Post-incident review notes
    

### Key Takeaway

Unusual device behavior may be an early warning sign of compromise.

Employees should recognize suspicious signs, stop risky activity, and report quickly. Managers should support reporting without blame. Executives should verify that endpoint protection, alerting, escalation, and response processes are working.

**Do not investigate quietly. Report early.**
