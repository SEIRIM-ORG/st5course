When most people think of phishing and social engineering attacks, we jump to two predominant examples: email and SMS messages. These medium are among the most common, but hackers are saturating every available avenue of approach through your personal and corporate defenses. 

In today's world of AI supercharging attackers efforts - be cognizant that the old methods of looking for misspellings and bad grammar in the communication isn't enough, now with little effort they are able to make messages that look professional, use correct grammar, and appear to come from a legitimate account while still being malicious.

## Everything, Everywhere All at Once


**Be aware of the following examples of other mediums and attack vectors through which social engineering attacks can occur:**
<br>
<br>

    

1. **Email** — corporate email, personal email, vendor email, fake notifications.
    
2. **SMS / text message** — mobile carrier texts, delivery alerts, bank alerts, verification-code scams.
    
3. **Phone calls** — direct voice calls to employees, executives, help desks, receptionists, or customers.
    
4. **Social media DMs** — LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok, etc.
    
5. **Messaging apps** — WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Messenger, Viber, WeChat.
    
6. **Collaboration platforms** — Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Google Chat.
    
7. **Video conferencing platforms** — Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams meetings, fake meeting invites.
    
8. **Websites / landing pages** — fake login portals, cloned vendor sites, fake HR portals, fake payment pages.
    
9. **QR codes** — printed QR codes, email QR codes, parking QR codes, restaurant/menu QR codes, event QR codes.
    
10. **Physical mail** — letters, fake invoices, legal notices, bank notices, USB mailers, package inserts.
    
11. **USB devices / removable media** — dropped USB drives, mailed flash drives, external hard drives, SD cards.
    
12. **In-person interaction** — office visits, front desk approaches, conferences, trade shows, fake contractors.
    
13. **Physical access points** — building entrances, reception areas, loading docks, elevators, server rooms.
    
14. **Job/recruitment platforms** — LinkedIn jobs, Indeed, Upwork, Fiverr, fake recruiter emails, fake applicant resumes.
    
15. **Customer support / help desk channels** — live chat, support tickets, password reset workflows, account recovery calls.


The takeaway here is that a cybersecurity attack can come from any and every angle. We must all always be skeptical of every bit of incoming communication, information, media and more.

### Remember to be vigilant across multiple vector types:

- **Digital vectors:** email, SMS, phone, social media, messaging apps, collaboration tools, video meetings, websites, QR codes, support portals.

- **Physical-world vectors:** in-person contact, mail, USB/media drops, building access points, events/conferences.
