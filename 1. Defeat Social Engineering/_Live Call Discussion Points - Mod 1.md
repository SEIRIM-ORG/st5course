### Discussion Scenario

An accounts payable employee receives this email:

“Hi, I am traveling today and cannot take calls. Please update the bank details for our next payment to Meridian Supply. The new account details are attached. This is urgent because the payment must go out before 4 PM. Please keep this between us until I am back online.”

Ask learners:

1. What psychological triggers are being used?
2. What makes this request high-risk?
3. What should the employee avoid doing?
4. What separate channel should be used?
5. What should be reported or documented?

Expected answer:

The message uses urgency, secrecy, authority, and pressure. It is high-risk because it involves a supplier payment and bank-detail change. The employee should not reply to the message, open the attachment, or update the records based only on the email. They should verify through the approved vendor record or internal finance process, using known contact details. If suspicious, they should report the message and document the attempted change.

### Short Knowledge Check

**Question:** A senior manager emails you from what appears to be their normal account and asks you to send a confidential file to a new external email address. What should you do?

A. Send it because the email came from the manager’s account  
B. Reply to the email and ask if it is real  
C. Verify through a separate trusted channel before sending anything  
D. Forward the file to a coworker and ask for their opinion

**Correct answer:** C

### Practical Exercise

Ask learners to review these three requests and decide whether each should require separate verification.

#### Request 1

“Can you send me the team lunch menu?”

Expected answer:

Low risk. Verification may not be required unless the message contains a suspicious link or attachment.

#### Request 2

“Please update this supplier’s bank account before today’s payment run.”

Expected answer:

High risk. Requires separate verification and documented approval.

#### Request 3

“I am locked out. Please approve the MFA request I just sent you.”

Expected answer:

High risk. Do not approve. Report immediately.

### Manager and Executive Exercise

Ask managers and executives to answer:

1. Which company processes could lead to financial loss if one employee is tricked?
    
2. Which processes could expose sensitive data if one employee is pressured?
    
3. Which departments are most likely to receive high-risk social engineering requests?
    
4. Which procedures currently rely too heavily on email approval?
    
5. Which high-risk changes require independent verification?
    
6. Are exceptions documented and reviewed?
    
7. Would employees feel safe challenging a suspicious request from an executive?
    

This exercise helps leadership identify weak points before attackers do.
