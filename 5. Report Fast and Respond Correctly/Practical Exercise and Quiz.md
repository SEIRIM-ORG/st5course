### Know What Counts as an Incident


### Short Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** When should employees report a suspicious cyber event?

A. Only after proving it is a breach  
B. Only if data is definitely stolen  
C. As soon as something seems suspicious or risky  
D. Only at the end of the week

**Correct answer:** C

**Question 2:** Which of the following should be reported?

A. Unexpected MFA prompts  
B. A file sent to the wrong person  
C. A lost work laptop  
D. All of the above

**Correct answer:** D

**Question 3:** What should employees do after clicking a suspicious link?

A. Hide it if nothing happened  
B. Report it immediately  
C. Delete all evidence  
D. Try to investigate the website

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 4:** What should leadership verify?

A. Whether employees know what to report and can report quickly  
B. Whether employees wait until they are certain  
C. Whether mistakes are punished before investigation  
D. Whether incident reports are discouraged

**Correct answer:** A

### Know How to Report


### Short Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** What is the best incident report?

A. A vague message saying “something weird happened”  
B. A clear report with facts about what happened, when, and what action was taken  
C. A report sent one week later  
D. A report only after proof of a breach

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 2:** What should you do after accidentally sending sensitive data to the wrong person?

A. Wait to see if anyone notices  
B. Report it immediately  
C. Delete your sent email and say nothing  
D. Ask the recipient to keep it secret without telling anyone

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 3:** What should you avoid doing before reporting a suspicious email?

A. Preserving the message  
B. Using the phishing report button  
C. Deleting the email before it is reviewed  
D. Explaining whether you clicked anything

**Correct answer:** C

**Question 4:** What should leadership verify?

A. Whether reporting channels are clear, tested, and available when needed  
B. Whether employees are afraid to report  
C. Whether reports disappear into inboxes  
D. Whether only IT knows the process

**Correct answer:** A

### Take the Correct First Actions


### Short Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** What are the correct first actions during a suspected incident?

A. Delete, restart, ignore, continue  
B. Stop, preserve, report, follow instructions  
C. Investigate, forward, post, delete

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 2:** Why should employees preserve suspicious emails or files?

A. To help the response team investigate  
B. To share them with coworkers  
C. To delete them later without reporting  
D. To prove nothing happened

**Correct answer:** A

**Question 3:** What should you do after entering a password into a suspicious page?

A. Report immediately and follow instructions  
B. Keep trying the page

**Correct answer:** A

**Question 4:** What should leadership verify?

A. Whether first-response actions, escalation paths, and playbooks are clear and tested

C. Whether evidence is deleted quickly

**Correct answer:** A

### Prepare and Exercise the Incident Response Plan


### Short Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** Why is a written incident response plan important?

A. It eliminates all incidents  
B. It defines roles, escalation, communication, and response steps before pressure begins  
C. It replaces security tools  
D. It is only useful for large companies

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 2:** What is the purpose of a tabletop exercise?

A. To blame employees for mistakes  
B. To test the response plan against a realistic scenario before a real incident  
C. To avoid updating the plan  
D. To prove IT can handle everything alone

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 3:** Which group should be involved in incident response planning?

A. Only IT  
B. Only executives  
C. IT, leadership, legal, communications, business owners, and relevant providers  
D. Only regular office staff

**Correct answer:** C

**Question 4:** What should happen after a tabletop exercise?

A. Nothing unless there was a real incident  
B. Findings should be documented, owners assigned, and improvements tracked  
C. The plan should be deleted  
D. Results should be hidden from leadership

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 5:** What should leadership verify?

A. Whether the plan is written, approved, tested, updated, and usable under pressure  
B. Whether the plan is long  
C. Whether only IT has seen it  
D. Whether exercises are avoided to save time

**Correct answer:** A

### Ensure Backups and Redundancies are Rock Solid


### Short Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** Why are backups important in incident response?

A. They prevent every cyberattack  
B. They may allow the company to restore data and systems after loss, corruption, or ransomware  
C. They replace employee reporting  
D. They make security updates unnecessary

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 2:** What proves that a backup is useful?

A. The backup vendor says it exists  
B. The backup has been restored and verified successfully  
C. The company assumes cloud data is safe  
D. The backup is listed in a spreadsheet only

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 3:** Why should backups be protected from ransomware?

A. Ransomware may try to delete, encrypt, or corrupt accessible backups  
B. Backups are never targeted  
C. Backups do not contain useful data  
D. Backup protection only matters for large companies

**Correct answer:** A

**Question 4:** What is redundancy?

A. A previous copy of a file  
B. Extra systems, channels, devices, providers, or processes that help the business continue operating  
C. A password reset  
D. A phishing email report

**Correct answer:** B

**Question 5:** What should leadership verify?

A. Whether backups are complete, protected, monitored, tested, and aligned to recovery needs  
B. Whether backups are assumed to work  
C. Whether only one person knows how recovery works  
D. Whether restore testing can be skipped

**Correct answer:** A
