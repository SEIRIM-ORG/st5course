### Goal 

This isn't just a glossary, understanding each of these concepts is key to successful cybersecurity.

## A

**Acceptable Use Policy**  
A company rule that explains how employees may use company devices, accounts, networks, software, email, internet access, and data.

**Access Control**  
The process of deciding who is allowed to access a system, file, application, device, or dataset.

**Account Compromise**  
A situation where an attacker gains access to a legitimate user account, often through stolen passwords, phishing, malware, or MFA manipulation.

**Administrator Account**  
A powerful account with the ability to change system settings, install software, manage users, or access sensitive areas. These accounts require stronger protection.

**After-Action Review**  
A structured review after an incident or exercise to identify what worked, what failed, and what should be improved.

**Alert**  
A warning generated by a security tool, system, or user report that may indicate suspicious activity, misconfiguration, malware, or unauthorized access.

**Approved Device**  
A work device that the company knows about, authorizes, manages, secures, and can remove access from when needed.

**Approved Software**  
Software, apps, cloud tools, or services that the company has reviewed and allowed for business use.

**Asset**  
Anything valuable to the company that needs protection. Assets may include devices, data, accounts, software, systems, people, suppliers, and business processes.

**Asset Inventory**  
A list of company assets, such as laptops, phones, servers, software, cloud systems, data locations, accounts, and business-critical tools.

**Attack Surface**  
All the places an attacker could try to enter, misuse, disrupt, or steal from a company. This includes accounts, devices, software, cloud services, email, vendors, and people.

**Attack Vector**  
The path or method an attacker uses to carry out an attack, such as phishing email, stolen credentials, exposed remote access, malware, or a vulnerable website.

**Authentication**  
The process of proving identity before accessing a system. Passwords, MFA, biometrics, passkeys, and security keys are examples of authentication methods.

**Authorization**  
The permission granted after authentication. Authentication proves who you are; authorization determines what you are allowed to access.

## B

**Backup**  
A recoverable copy of data or systems that can be used after deletion, corruption, ransomware, device failure, or other disruption.

**Backup Encryption**  
The protection of backup data so unauthorized people cannot read it if the backup is stolen or exposed.

**Backup Monitoring**  
Checking whether backups are completing successfully, failing, missing, corrupted, or behaving unusually.

**Backup Restore Test**  
A test that confirms backup data can actually be restored and used. A backup is not proven until it has been restored successfully.

**Bring Your Own Device**  
A policy or practice where employees use personal devices for work. This creates risk if the devices are not managed, secured, and governed properly.

**Browser Extension**  
A small software add-on installed in a web browser. Extensions can be useful, but some may access web pages, collect data, or introduce risk.

**Brute Force Attack**  
An attack where an attacker repeatedly guesses passwords or login combinations until access is gained.

**Business Continuity**  
The ability of the company to keep operating during or after disruption, such as cyberattack, outage, system failure, or loss of critical staff.

**Business Email Compromise**  
A fraud attack where criminals use email impersonation or compromised accounts to trick employees into sending money, changing payment details, or sharing sensitive information.

## C

**Call Tree**  
A predefined list of people to contact during an emergency, usually with backup contacts and escalation order.

**CIA Triad**  
A cybersecurity model based on confidentiality, integrity, and availability. It helps explain what cybersecurity is trying to protect.

**Clean Desk Practice**  
The habit of keeping sensitive papers, notes, devices, and storage media secured instead of leaving them exposed on desks or in meeting rooms.

**Cloud Service**  
A technology service accessed over the internet, such as email, file storage, CRM, accounting systems, HR platforms, or collaboration tools.

**Cloud Storage**  
Online file storage, such as company-approved document platforms. Cloud storage must still be configured, controlled, and backed up appropriately.

**Compensating Control**  
An alternative security measure used when the preferred control cannot be applied. For example, extra monitoring may be used when a system cannot be patched immediately.

**Compromise**  
A situation where an account, device, system, or data has been accessed, changed, controlled, or exposed without authorization.

**Confidential Information**  
Information that should not be shared publicly and may harm the company, customers, employees, or partners if exposed.

**Configuration Baseline**  
The approved secure setup for devices, systems, software, or cloud services. It defines how systems should be configured by default.

**Configuration Drift**  
The gradual movement away from approved secure settings over time, often caused by exceptions, manual changes, updates, or unmanaged tools.

**Containment**  
The process of limiting the spread or damage of an incident, such as disabling accounts, isolating devices, blocking links, or restricting access.

**Credential**  
Information or a method used to prove identity. Examples include passwords, passkeys, MFA codes, security keys, recovery codes, and API keys.

**Credential Harvesting**  
A phishing or scam technique designed to trick people into entering passwords, MFA codes, or other login information.

**Critical System**  
A system the business depends on heavily, such as email, finance, payroll, customer records, order processing, production systems, or identity management.

**Cyber Hygiene**  
Basic security habits that reduce everyday cyber risk, such as using MFA, updating software, reporting suspicious messages, and using approved tools.

**Cyber Incident**  
An event that may involve unauthorized access, data exposure, malware, account compromise, fraud, service disruption, or violation of security procedures.

**Cyber Insurance**  
Insurance that may help cover certain cyber incident costs, subject to policy terms, exclusions, notification duties, and required controls.

**Cyber Risk**  
The possibility that cyber threats could harm the company’s systems, data, operations, finances, customers, reputation, or legal position.

**Cybersecurity Governance**  
The leadership structure for assigning ownership, making decisions, reviewing evidence, managing risk, and ensuring cybersecurity work actually happens.

## D

**Data Breach**  
An incident where sensitive, protected, or confidential information is accessed, disclosed, copied, stolen, or exposed without authorization.

**Data Classification**  
The process of labeling information by sensitivity, such as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted.

**Data Exposure**  
A situation where information becomes accessible to people, systems, or services that should not have access to it.

**Data Loss Prevention**  
Controls designed to detect, warn, restrict, or block unsafe sharing, uploading, downloading, or transfer of sensitive data.

**Data Minimization**  
Using, sharing, collecting, or storing only the amount of data needed for a legitimate purpose.

**Data Owner**  
The person or role responsible for deciding how a dataset should be used, protected, shared, retained, and deleted.

**Data Retention**  
Rules for how long information should be kept before it is archived or deleted.

**Decryption**  
The process of turning encrypted information back into readable form using the proper key or authorization.

**Default Password**  
A password that comes preset on a device, account, system, or application. Default passwords should be changed before use.

**Digital Forensics**  
The process of collecting and analyzing digital evidence after an incident to understand what happened.

**Disaster Recovery**  
The process and capability for restoring systems, data, infrastructure, and operations after a major disruption.

**DMARC**  
An email authentication control that helps prevent attackers from spoofing a company’s domain in email.

**Domain Spoofing**  
An impersonation technique where an attacker makes an email or website appear to come from a trusted domain.

## E

**Email Filtering**  
A security control that attempts to block or warn about spam, phishing, malware, suspicious links, or unsafe attachments.

**Encryption**  
The process of protecting information by making it unreadable without the proper key or authorization.

**Endpoint**  
A device that connects to company systems, such as a laptop, desktop, phone, tablet, or server.

**Endpoint Detection and Response**  
A security tool or service that monitors devices for suspicious activity and supports detection, investigation, and response.

**Endpoint Protection**  
Security software that helps protect devices from malware, suspicious behavior, and other threats.

**Escalation**  
The process of moving an issue to the right person or team with the authority or expertise to handle it.

**Exception Register**  
A list of approved exceptions to normal security rules, including owner, reason, risk, expiry date, and compensating controls.

**Exploit**  
A method, code, or technique used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

**External Sharing**  
Sharing files, folders, systems, or data with people outside the company, such as customers, vendors, contractors, or partners.

## F

**Firewall**  
A security control that helps filter network traffic and block unauthorized or risky connections.

**Fraud-Resistant Process**  
A business process designed so that one message, one person, or one rushed approval cannot cause major harm.

## G

**Governance Function**  
The leadership and oversight part of cybersecurity that sets expectations, roles, accountability, risk decisions, policies, and review processes.

## H

**Hardening**  
The process of securely configuring devices, software, systems, and cloud services to reduce unnecessary risk.

**Help Desk Impersonation**  
A social engineering tactic where an attacker pretends to be an employee or trusted person to trick support staff into resetting credentials or access.

## I

**Identity and Access Management**  
The process of managing digital identities, accounts, permissions, authentication, authorization, and access reviews.

**Immutable Backup**  
A backup that cannot be changed or deleted for a defined period, making it harder for ransomware or attackers to destroy.

**Incident Lead**  
The person responsible for coordinating the company’s response during a cybersecurity incident.

**Incident Response**  
The organized process of identifying, reporting, containing, investigating, communicating, recovering from, and learning from a cybersecurity incident.

**Incident Response Plan**  
A written plan that defines roles, reporting channels, escalation paths, playbooks, communication rules, and decision authority for cyber incidents.

**Indicator of Compromise**  
A sign that a system, account, or device may have been compromised, such as suspicious login activity, unusual files, or known malicious IP addresses.

**Information Security**  
The protection of information from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, destruction, or disruption.

**Insider Threat**  
A risk that comes from someone inside or connected to the organization, such as an employee, contractor, or trusted provider.

**Integrity**  
The accuracy, completeness, and trustworthiness of data or systems. If information is changed without authorization, integrity is harmed.

**Internet-Facing System**  
A system, website, application, remote-access service, or device that can be reached from the public internet.

## J

**Joiner-Mover-Leaver Process**  
The process for creating access when someone joins, changing access when someone changes roles, and removing access when someone leaves.

## K

**Keylogger**  
Malware or monitoring software that records keystrokes, potentially stealing passwords, messages, or sensitive information.

## L

**Least Privilege**  
The principle that people and systems should have only the access they need to perform their role, and no more.

**Legacy System**  
An older system, device, or application that may still be used but may be harder to secure, patch, support, or replace.

**Local Administrator Rights**  
High-level permissions on a device that allow a user to install software, change settings, or modify system-level controls.

**Log**  
A record of system, user, security, or application activity. Logs help investigate incidents and monitor suspicious behavior.

## M

**Malware**  
Malicious software designed to harm, disrupt, spy on, steal from, or control systems or data.

**Managed Detection and Response**  
An outsourced security service that monitors threats, investigates alerts, and helps respond to suspicious activity.

**Managed Service Provider**  
An external provider that manages IT services such as devices, networks, cloud systems, backups, user accounts, or support.

**Manual Fallback Procedure**  
A predefined manual process used when normal systems are unavailable, such as manual order tracking or emergency customer communication.

**MFA Fatigue**  
An attack where repeated MFA prompts are sent to a user in the hope that the user approves one by mistake.

**Mobile Device Management**  
Technology used to manage, secure, configure, monitor, lock, or wipe mobile devices such as phones and tablets.

**Multi-Factor Authentication**  
A login method that requires more than one proof of identity, such as a password plus an authenticator app, passkey, or security key.

## N

**Near Miss**  
A suspicious event or mistake that could have become an incident but was caught or reported before major harm occurred.

**Network Segmentation**  
Separating parts of a network so that compromise of one area does not automatically give access to everything else.

## O

**Offline Backup**  
A backup that is not continuously connected to the network or production systems, making it harder for attackers to alter or delete.

**Offboarding**  
The process of removing access, collecting devices, disabling accounts, and transferring work when someone leaves the company or no longer needs access.

**Open Risk**  
A known risk that has not yet been resolved, transferred, avoided, or formally accepted.

## P

**Passkey**  
A modern authentication method that can replace or reduce reliance on passwords, often using device-based cryptographic authentication.

**Password Manager**  
A tool that securely stores and manages passwords, helping users create unique passwords for each account.

**Patch**  
A software update that fixes a bug, security weakness, or performance issue.

**Patch Management**  
The process of identifying, prioritizing, testing, installing, and verifying software and system updates.

**Phishing**  
A deceptive message designed to trick someone into clicking a link, opening a file, entering credentials, sending money, or sharing information.

**Phishing-Resistant MFA**  
Authentication designed to resist common phishing attacks, often using passkeys, FIDO security keys, or similar methods.

**Policy**  
A written rule that defines expected behavior, responsibilities, and requirements.

**Privileged Account**  
An account with elevated access to systems, data, security settings, user administration, or infrastructure.

**Public Link**  
A file-sharing link that may allow access without proper restriction. Public links can expose sensitive data if used incorrectly.

## Q

**QR Code Phishing**  
A phishing technique that uses QR codes to send users to fake login pages or malicious websites.

## R

**Ransomware**  
Malware that encrypts or blocks access to files or systems and demands payment for restoration or non-disclosure.

**Recovery Point Objective**  
The maximum amount of data loss the business can tolerate, measured by how far back restored data may be.

**Recovery Time Objective**  
The maximum amount of time the business can tolerate a system being unavailable before serious impact occurs.

**Redundancy**  
Extra systems, providers, devices, communication methods, or processes that help the business continue operating if something fails.

**Remote Access**  
A method for accessing company systems from outside the office. Remote access must be approved, secured, and monitored.

**Remediation**  
The action taken to fix, reduce, or control a vulnerability, incident cause, misconfiguration, or security weakness.

**Restore**  
The process of bringing data, files, systems, or services back from backup.

**Risk Acceptance**  
A formal decision to accept a known risk, usually with documented reasoning, approval, review date, and ownership.

**Risk Register**  
A list of known risks, owners, severity, treatment plans, status, and review dates.

**Role-Based Access Control**  
A method of assigning access based on job role rather than individual preference or informal requests.

## S

**SaaS**  
Software as a Service. Cloud-based software accessed through a browser or app, such as CRM, email, accounting, HR, or project-management systems.

**Screen Lock**  
A control that prevents someone from using a device after the user steps away.

**Security Awareness Training**  
Training that teaches employees how to recognize cyber risks and follow safe behaviors.

**Security Baseline**  
The minimum approved set of security settings, tools, and configurations for a device, system, or service.

**Security Information and Event Management**  
A system that collects and analyzes logs and alerts from multiple sources to help detect suspicious activity.

**Security Key**  
A physical authentication device used to verify identity, often as a strong form of MFA.

**Shadow AI**  
The use of AI tools without company approval, often involving hidden data exposure or compliance risk.

**Shadow IT**  
The use of devices, software, cloud services, or tools without company approval or visibility.

**Shoulder Surfing**  
Watching someone’s screen, keyboard, notes, or device to obtain confidential information.

**Social Engineering**  
Manipulating people into taking unsafe actions, such as clicking links, sharing credentials, approving payments, or bypassing procedures.

**Spear Phishing**  
A targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific person, team, company, or role.

**Spoofing**  
Making a message, caller ID, website, or identity appear to come from a trusted source.

**Strong Password**  
A password that is long, unique, not reused, difficult to guess, and stored safely.

**Supplier Risk**  
Cybersecurity or business risk introduced through vendors, contractors, suppliers, cloud services, or managed providers.

## T

**Tabletop Exercise**  
A discussion-based exercise where participants walk through a realistic incident scenario to test roles, decisions, escalation, and response plans.

**Threat**  
Anything that could harm systems, data, people, operations, or business processes.

**Threat Actor**  
A person, group, or organization that carries out or attempts cyberattacks.

**Triage**  
The process of quickly assessing a report, alert, or incident to determine severity, priority, and next actions.

**Two-Person Approval**  
A control requiring two authorized people to approve a sensitive action, such as payment release or vendor bank-detail changes.

## U

**Unauthorized Access**  
Access to systems, accounts, files, or data without proper permission.

**Unsupported Software**  
Software that no longer receives security updates or vendor support. Unsupported software increases risk.

**USB Risk**  
The risk created by USB drives, cables, or ports that may introduce malware, copy data, or connect unknown devices.

**User Account**  
A digital identity assigned to a person or system for accessing company resources.

## V

**Vendor Bank-Detail Fraud**  
A fraud technique where attackers trick a company into changing supplier payment details.

**Vulnerability**  
A weakness in software, hardware, configuration, process, or behavior that could be exploited.

**Vulnerability Management**  
The process of finding, prioritizing, assigning, fixing, mitigating, and verifying closure of vulnerabilities.

**Vulnerability Scanning**  
Using tools to identify known weaknesses, missing patches, misconfigurations, exposed systems, or risky software.

## W

**Web Filtering**  
A control that blocks or warns users about risky, malicious, inappropriate, or unauthorized websites.

**Whaling**  
A targeted phishing or fraud attack aimed at executives or senior decision-makers.

**Workaround**  
An unofficial method used to bypass a problem or control. Workarounds may create hidden security risk if not approved.

## Z

**Zero-Day Vulnerability**  
A vulnerability that is not yet publicly fixed or may not be known to the vendor when attackers begin using it.

**Zero Trust**  
A security approach that does not automatically trust users, devices, networks, or systems. Access must be verified and limited.

**Zombie Account**  
An old, forgotten, or inactive account that still has access and may be abused if not disabled.
